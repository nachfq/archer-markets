# Arbitrum signals and event requirements

## Scope of the conclusions

The evidence reviewed as of September 9, 2026 distinguishes four levels: Singapore buildathon requirements, Founder House guidance, ecosystem technical priorities, and precedents from other editions. An infrastructure priority does not demonstrate commercial demand for an application. A London winner does not demonstrate that its vertical has better odds in Singapore either.

## The online event

HackQuest lists a competition open to different categories, with mandatory deployment on an Arbitrum chain; its examples of eligible networks include testnets. Evaluation mentions contract quality, potential to attract and retain users, innovation, and addressing real needs. It does not publish numerical weights. The announced pool is USD 115,000: 70,000 for general prizes, 15,000 for Promising Products, and 30,000 in grants. There are reserved places for Robinhood Chain and Arbitrum projects. General prizes are subject to development milestones. These details come from the listing, not a reading of all legal terms. [^1]

The organizer's communications place the buildathon between September 14 and October 4; Founder House takes place October 23–25. Online and in-person applications are independent. Detailed HackQuest times must be checked in the account's time zone before setting the operational calendar. [^1][^2]

The AI Agentic category published for London should not be imported automatically: the current Singapore listing uses Promising Products. Nor should HackQuest's AI preparation section be interpreted as complete authorization for every form of automated participation. [^1][^3]

The linked Singapore terms PDF could not be retrieved: it returned 403 through web reading and 429 through a direct HTTP request. Detailed rules on the human team, AI contributions, geographic eligibility, intellectual property, preexisting material, and deliverables remain unresolved. This limitation does not prevent comparing verticals; it does prevent declaring eligibility settled. [^4]

## Which verticals the team suggests

Founder House Singapore lists stablecoins/payments, tokenization/RWA, DeFi/financial infrastructure, AI/agentic finance, privacy, consumer products, and developer tools. The list does not establish a ranking. The same page describes interest in products with execution potential and ecosystem alignment. [^2]

The financial emphasis is more pronounced in the August buildathon announcement, which invites teams with new financial products. This is a relevant editorial signal, consistent with HackQuest's open scope; it does not justify excluding consumer products or tools. [^5]

| Evidence level | Appropriate use | Inference it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore requirements | Check admissibility and design the submission | Invent weights or a probability of winning |
| Founder House verticals | Guide exploration of users and problems | Assign a prize to each vertical |
| Arbitrum technical publications | Identify friction and capabilities of interest to the ecosystem | Claim that a customer is willing to pay |
| Previous winners | Understand the specificity of supported proposals | Copy a solution or extrapolate success |
| DAO RFPs and reports | Find needs of specific buyers | Present them as requests from the hackathon organizer |

## Signals more specific than a list of themes

The June 15 architecture publication describes selective confidentiality, predictable costs, controls for dedicated chains, and settlement improvements. The need for authorized third parties to review confidential activity offers a more specific direction than “build something with privacy.” Several capabilities appear as under development in that publication. This report uses it as a signal of intent, not an inventory of features available today. Each chosen dependency will need documentation and a current test. [^6]

In AI, Ben Greenberg devotes an article to inference verification and the distinction between a provider's assertion and checkable evidence. He describes payments as infrastructure and directs attention toward verifying what the agent did. This is an explicit invitation to explore trust in AI associated with Founder House London, not a confirmed Singapore category. It also does not support treating a signed log as proof that a model executed correctly. [^7]

The Foundation has also publicized an LG Electronics pilot for recording verifiable advertising performance. This is evidence of enterprise exploration beyond conventional financial interfaces. An announced pilot does not demonstrate mass adoption or imply that the advertising-measurement market is unoccupied. [^8]

**Analytical judgment:** the most useful signals are cross-cutting problems—verification, selective visibility, coordination, and infrastructure operations—that can occur across several verticals. It is better to compare specific user tasks than to choose a broad label and generate ideas within it.

## Lessons from previous editions

For the NYC online buildathon, the Foundation highlighted product clarity, technical execution, alignment, and continuity. It identified Tilt Protocol, Fangorn, and EqualFi as winners. The article does not provide enough detail to attribute their wins to particular features or reconstruct the evaluation. [^9]

Founder House London recognized solutions in institutional collateral (Liquida), risk transfer (The Risk Protocol), finance for Africa and the diaspora (EdenFi), and equity management (Obolos). It also awarded trading and privacy agents, and ReineiraOS for capital-backed commitments and compensation for breaches. [^10]

The commercial lesson is that a specific user and process support a more defensible explanation than “a Web3 platform for everyone.” The competitive lesson is equally significant: generic agent accountability already appears among supported projects. New proposals must be compared with those solutions. This precedent describes an in-person program for teams already building; their scope is not a reasonable benchmark for what a new team can finish in three weeks.

## Implications for product selection

The choice must pass three independent checks. First, a problem supported by an operational report, documented limitation, concrete request, or user research. Second, a difference that survives a review of current tools. Third, a prototype that reproduces the main task and uses Arbitrum for a verifiable function.

The ability to build a demo is not enough to demonstrate a business. A large market is not enough either if the prototype requires an issuer, custodian, organization, or provider that is not yet collaborating. The study compares product opportunities with those dependencies in mind, without assigning commercial value to an integration alone.

Transparency about agent contributions can strengthen project documentation. It does not appear as an independent evaluation criterion in the reviewed sources. Its contribution should be demonstrated through reproducible decisions, implementation, and review, while keeping the identities of human participants distinct.
